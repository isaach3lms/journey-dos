{# Starting notifications, and telling the app who is signed in. The wrapper around this site loads the live pages rather than bundled files, so the web layer the provider's SDK expects to be started from is this one. That is the whole design: the wrapper's only job is to exist, and everything else is a deploy here rather than a rebuild, a resubmission and an App Store review. Three calls, each run at a different rhythm and each for a reason: `initialize` once per app launch. Repeating it on every page would be harmless but pointless, and sessionStorage is exactly the lifetime of the app's web view. `login` on every page, because it is the one that can be wrong. A member who signs out and hands their phone to their spouse has to stop receiving the first person's notifications, and the wrapper has no idea either of those things happened. Setting it on every load means the id is always whoever is signed in now. `requestPermission` once per app launch, and never on the sign-in screen, because iOS shows its prompt exactly once ever. Asking somebody who has not got into the app yet spends that one chance on a stranger. Nothing happens in an ordinary browser: there is no SDK there to call, the member app works normally, and the only thing missing is a notification a browser could not have shown anyway. #} {% if push_app_id %} {% endif %}
